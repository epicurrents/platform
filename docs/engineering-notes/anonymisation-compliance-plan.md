# Anonymisation compliance — execution plan

**Status: v1.2, 2026-09-22. Phases 0 and 1 shipped; phases 2–7 not started.** Three decisions taken on review are folded in: `file_hash` leaves every response, the author's included; a centre's withdrawal confirmation is the purge command's report and never a lookup; and the dataset releases on a monthly cadence, with the acquisition embargo's length left to the deployment. The reason this plan exists is the edu project's multi-centre teaching dataset, whose design in the project's own engineering note is a contextual-approach anonymisation case; [the section on it](#the-edu-teaching-dataset-under-the-guidelines) says what the guidelines require of that design and which phases are prerequisites for its first release. The assessment in [docs/anonymisation-compliance.md](../anonymisation-compliance.md) found that the platform's de-identified output is pseudonymised personal data under the EDPB Guidelines 02/2026 on Anonymisation, and recorded eight design gaps. This note is the plan for closing the ones the software can close, in an order where each phase leaves the platform in a consistent, shippable state. Paragraph references (¶ *n*) are to the guidelines' consultation version, as in the assessment.

## What "comply in the technical sense" means here

The guidelines do not require anonymisation. They require that data described as anonymous actually be anonymous (¶ 40), that the anonymising controller be able to demonstrate what was done and that it worked (¶ 41), that the assessment be made from each relevant entity's perspective (¶ 11–12), and that it be repeated when capabilities or circumstances change (¶ 35, 42, 88). A platform that serves pseudonymised data complies by not overclaiming, by not creating linkage it need not create, by recording per recording what it did, and by giving a deployment the means to make and keep a per-recipient assessment. That is the scope of this plan.

Two things it does not attempt, stated so nobody reads their absence as an oversight. **The signal is not anonymisable at full resolution.** A recording matches its source copy bit-for-bit and by correlation after any transformation short of destroying it, and the state of the art in EEG subject identification is a means the assessment has to assume is available to a recipient holding a reference recording (¶ 84, 92). The only genuinely anonymous product this platform could ever emit is a derived, aggregated one, which is a feature that does not exist and is not in this plan. **Media and DICOM** have no de-identification and are personal data by inspection (¶ 50); they keep their existing ROADMAP entries.

## Phase 0 — Vocabulary (shipped 2026-09-22)

The rule is in AGENTS.md under de-identification: served output is de-identified or pseudonymised, never anonymous. Applied across code, tests, UI strings and documentation. `AnonymizeEDFHeader` is now `DeidentifyEDFHeader`; the old name stays importable as an alias in [federation/middleware.py](../../federation/middleware.py) because project pipelines are separate repositories that import it, and the EDF middleware ABCs are on the platform's compatibility surface. Three user-facing strings changed (the share-token panel, the upload form's display-name hint, the API error for a FAILED recording requested under a de-identifying grant). The published data-protection page was edited in the docs submodule and goes out with its next release. The nested project repository under `projects/` was deliberately left alone: its course material discusses anonymisation as a concept, which the rule does not cover, and its own claims about platform output are its own commit.

## Phase 1 — Close the platform-created linkage keys (shipped 2026-09-22)

Shipped as written below, with two details decided in the doing. `refresh_signal_metadata` compares the file's digest to `stored_hash` as well as the header to the metadata rows, so a rewrite that changes samples but not the header is noticed and corrected on its own audit row; `backfill_stored_hash` exists for the non-EDF READY recordings the refresh never looks at and for a cheaper sweep. The upload response lost `file_hash` too and gained nothing, since `stored_hash` does not exist until processing has run. The load-bearing registry row is on the serialisers rather than on the test file.

**Why first.** Every other phase, and any contextual assessment a deployment might make, is void while the metadata response hands a recipient a bit-exact key to the original (¶ 62, 79) and a proxy for the acquisition date (¶ 63). These are the two gaps the platform created rather than inherited, and closing them costs one migration and a schema change.

**`file_hash`.** Today it is the SHA-256 of the upload before de-identification, computed from the upload stream and from the source file on import, and served in `RecordingOut` and the slice response to every reader.

- Remove it from `RecordingOut` and the slice response entirely, the author's view included: drop the field from the schemas in [recordings/api/v1/ninja.py](../../recordings/api/v1/ninja.py) rather than gating it. Nobody needs it on the wire. The author identifies an upload by `display_name` and the stored-bytes digest below, and the one legitimate question the hash answers, whether a given original is still on the platform, is answered by the purge report in Phase 7 and by nothing else, because an endpoint that answers it is the membership oracle of ¶ 79 whoever asks. The column stays for its internal uses (preservation manifest, the duplicate-detection ROADMAP entry, the dataset purge command).
- Add `Recording.stored_hash`: the SHA-256 of the stored bytes, computed at the end of `process_recording` after every in-place rewrite, and recomputed wherever the platform rewrites the file (`refresh_signal_metadata` is the existing re-derive-from-disk pattern; a project reprocessing stage calls the same helper). This is the public fingerprint: a digest of already-pseudonymised content links to nothing outside the platform. `content_hash` keeps its definition, since it folds in `file_hash` through a second SHA-256 and is not invertible to it.
- Frontend: the details panel in [HomeView.vue](../../frontend/src/views/HomeView.vue) shows `stored_hash`; the TypeScript types in [recordings.ts](../../frontend/src/api/recordings.ts) drop `file_hash`.
- Backfill: a management command computing `stored_hash` for existing READY rows from disk, idempotent, reported in [docs/management-commands.md](../management-commands.md). A deployment that has already served `file_hash` to recipients cannot recall it; the [assessment log](../anonymisation-compliance.md#assessment-log) records the date so a contextual assessment for a pre-existing grant knows to treat the recipient as holding it.

**`created_at`.** Served to every reader; on a clinical deployment it is the acquisition date within hours.

- For non-author readers, serve it truncated to the first of its month (UTC). That keeps listing order stable enough for a grantee's dataset view and removes the day-and-hour resolution that links to an acquisition log. Authors and superusers keep the exact value. Federated peers and share-token holders are non-authors by construction.
- `modified_at` is not served today and stays that way. `deleted_at` is serialised unconditionally but is null for every non-author reader by construction, since trashed recordings are gated out before any grant is read; the shape test below pins that rather than leaving it to reading.
- Push-notification bodies do not carry timestamps; no change.

**The shape-pinning contract test.** The reassessment trigger at ¶ 35 and ¶ 88 needs a mechanism, or the next `Out` field ships without anyone re-reading the given-data table. Add a `test_grantee_visible_shape` module beside the serve-pipeline parity test in [recordings/tests/](../../recordings/tests/): it serialises a recording for a grantee, a share-token holder and a federated peer and asserts the *exact* key set of the response and of each nested `meta` and `signals[]` object against a literal in the test, with a failure message naming the given-data table in the assessment. Adding a field then fails the suite until the developer updates both. This is the load-bearing pattern applied to the response shape rather than to a function; put the test on the load-bearing registry beside `_build_serve_pipeline`.

**Tests and reviews.** `TestServePipelineParity` unaffected. The access-matrix test in [epicurrents/tests/test_access_matrix.py](../../epicurrents/tests/test_access_matrix.py) gains a no-`file_hash`-key case and month-truncated `created_at` cases per caller, the author included for the first. `phi-exposure` (Out schema change), `gdpr-compliance` (new column), `audit-trail-completeness` (the backfill command opens a `with_system_activity` scope), `documentation-style`. The REST API is versioned by path and outside the platform pin, so no version bump; a CHANGELOG entry under the current patch.

**Docs.** [recordings/README.md](../../recordings/README.md) model table and de-identification list; the given-data table and gap table in the assessment; the [data-protection page](../epicurrents/src/docs/latest/platform/data-protection.md) in the docs submodule at the next release.

## Phase 2 — Safe model default for `apply_middleware`

**Why.** `AccessRight.apply_middleware` defaults to `False` on the model while the upload endpoint, the library grant schema and the federation service default it to `True`. A row created through the ORM by a project, a fixture or a data migration is a raw grant unless its author remembered the flag; the safe default lives one layer above the row.

- Flip the model default to `True` and reword the help text to name the explicit `False` as the deliberate act the federation services docstring already calls it. Migration is a default change only; no data is rewritten, since an existing row's stored value is its decision.
- The author's own `AccessRight` row created at upload is unaffected: the flag is ignored for authors on every serving path.
- Search every `AccessRight(` and `AccessRight.objects.create(` in core, [projects/example/](../../projects/example/) and the plugins for a call site that relied on the old default to mean raw; there should be none, and the search result goes in the commit message.
- Test: an `AccessRight` created with no `apply_middleware` argument serves de-identified bytes to a grantee (the reverse of the current implicit behaviour). Add it to [epicurrents/tests/test_permissions.py](../../epicurrents/tests/test_permissions.py) beside the terms-resolution cases.
- Reviews: `phi-exposure`, `load-bearing-diff-reviewer` if [epicurrents/permissions.py](../../epicurrents/permissions.py) is touched (it should not be), `gdpr-compliance`.

A project that creates grants in a data migration and expected raw serving will notice at its next test run, which is the point. Note it in the CHANGELOG as a behaviour change for project code.

## Phase 3 — Per-recording record of the de-identification applied

**Why.** ¶ 41: the controller documents the anonymisation process, including its testing, and keeps that documentation. Today `channel_order_version` is the only fact stamped per recording; whether annotation text was stripped or preserved is a transient task argument, and the ingest privacy overrides in force at the time are not recorded anywhere.

- `RecordingMeta.deidentification_version`: an integer stamped at ingest from a `DEIDENTIFICATION_VERSION` constant in [recordings/processors/edf.py](../../recordings/processors/edf.py), bumped by any change to `_build_clean_header` or `deidentify_signal_infos` (a test asserts the constant changes when either function's source digest changes, so the bump cannot be forgotten). Served to every reader like `channel_order_version`; `0` means a file processed before the field existed.
- `RecordingMeta.annotation_text_preserved`: boolean, stamped from the resolved `strip_annotation_text` decision. Served to every reader: under a de-identifying grant the wire strips text regardless, and under a raw grant the recipient is entitled to know the file carries it.
- The READY transition's audit row already embeds the `SignalInfo` digest in `extra_payload`; add the four ingest override values and the two new fields to the same payload. That puts the process record in the tamper-evident trail without a new table.
- `manage.py deidentification_report [--recording <hash>] [--format json]`: prints, per recording, the stamped versions, the annotation flag, the override values from the READY audit row, and the current constants, and flags any recording whose stamped version is behind the current one. This is the artefact the operator hands to a supervisory authority or attaches to a data-sharing agreement.
- Reprocessing: `refresh_signal_metadata` leaves both new fields alone, as it does `channel_order_version`, for the same reason: a refresh reads the header and cannot know which pass wrote it. A recording behind the current version is reprocessed only by a deliberate re-ingest, which stamps it.
- Reviews: `gdpr-compliance` (new columns, no personal data), `audit-trail-completeness` (payload change and the command's activity scope), `phi-exposure` (two new `Out` fields, and the Phase 1 shape test must be updated in the same commit, which is what it is for).

## Phase 4 — Free-text hygiene

**Why.** `display_name`, dataset names and descriptions, folder names and tag names are unvalidated and grantee-visible; tags are listed to every authenticated user regardless of access. Free text is the classic identifier channel (¶ 23, 50), and the notice has to say these fields are the uploader's responsibility until the platform at least warns.

- **Tag listing scope.** `list_tags` in [library/api/v1/ninja.py](../../library/api/v1/ninja.py) returns tags the caller created or that decorate an object the caller can read; superusers see all. The per-item access check the tag item listing already uses is the batching to reuse. This is the one item in the phase that is a control rather than a warning.
- **Identifier-shaped name warning.** A `looks_like_identifier(text)` helper in a new text-hygiene module in the core app: runs of six or more digits, date patterns in the common clinical spellings, `Surname, Firstname` and `Firstname Surname` shapes against a short capitalised-two-token heuristic, and strings matching the deployment's own identifier patterns from a `TEXT_HYGIENE_PATTERNS` setting a project can extend. The upload endpoint, the recording PATCH, the collection bulk-rename and the dataset and folder create and rename endpoints return a `warnings: [...]` list beside the result rather than refusing, because a false positive on a legitimate label must not block work. The frontend surfaces the warning as a confirm step on the field.
- **Push bodies.** [recordings/tasks.py](../../recordings/tasks.py) `_push_display_name` currently prefers `display_name`. Push bodies surface on lock screens and cross the Celery broker in plaintext; switch them to the hash-prefix handle unconditionally. The author knows which recording they uploaded; the label is one tap away in the app.
- **Notice.** [docs/privacy-notice-template.md](../privacy-notice-template.md) gains a sentence in the recording-subject notice that labels typed by the uploader are shown to recipients as typed.
- Reviews: `phi-exposure` (response shapes gain `warnings`), `csrf-coverage` and `audit-trail-completeness` if any endpoint is added rather than modified, `documentation-style`. Tests: the heuristic's own table-driven test, the tag-scope test per caller class, a push-body test asserting no `display_name` in the payload.

## Phase 5 — Label the annotation export and add a withholding mode

**Why.** [annotations/export.py](../../annotations/export.py) serves annotation text verbatim, gated by role rather than by grant, and is documented as the one path that answers past the text rule. That is a deliberate position, but the exported document leaves the platform and nothing in it says what it is.

- Add `"data_classification": "pseudonymised_personal_data"` and the source deployment's `deidentification_version` range to the export envelope, bump `FORMAT_VERSION`, and document the field in [annotations/README.md](../../annotations/README.md) so a recipient's tooling can refuse to treat the file as anonymous.
- `?withhold_text=true`: apply `withheld_row_ids` semantics to every row the caller did not write, as a de-identifying grant would, so an export prepared for a recipient under a de-identifying arrangement carries timing, codes and authorship without text. The role gate is unchanged; this is a narrower export, not a wider one.
- Reviews: `phi-exposure`, `audit-trail-completeness` (the export's activity metadata gains the flag), `documentation-style`. Tests in [annotations/tests/test_export.py](../../annotations/tests/test_export.py): the envelope fields, and the withholding mode leaving own rows readable and every other row's text empty.

## Phase 6 — Contextual-assessment support and reassessment triggers

**Why.** A deployment may reach "anonymous for this recipient" through the contextual approach (¶ 96), and the guidelines want that finding written down, kept with the data, and re-checked on defined triggers (¶ 41, 42, 88). The platform can hold the finding beside the grant and make the triggers observable; it cannot make the finding. This phase serves sharing between platform users. The edu dataset's finding attaches to a release run rather than to a grant, because its learners hold no per-recording grants; that record is Phase 7's.

- `AccessRight.assessment_reference` (a short text or URL, author-private) and `assessment_date`: where a sharer has a documented contextual assessment for a grant, the grant records it. Exposed on the grant listing and edit surfaces and in `explain_access`; never to the grantee. Registered for the Art. 15 export as the sharer's data.
- A `manage.py grant_assessments --older-than <days>` listing grants whose assessment predates a cut-off or predates the recording's current `deidentification_version`, for the six-monthly sweep.
- Incident trigger: [docs/operator-runbook.md](../operator-runbook.md) gains a step after any incident touching the originals volume, a backup, the database or a peer's credentials: list the grants with assessments and re-run them, per ¶ 42.
- `.review/agents/phi-exposure.md` gains a check: any change to `RecordingOut`, `RecordingMetaOut`, `SignalInfoOut` or a serialiser in [annotations/api/v1/ninja.py](../../annotations/api/v1/ninja.py) must touch the given-data table in the assessment in the same diff. The Phase 1 shape test enforces the same thing mechanically; the agent check catches the annotation side, which the shape test does not cover.
- Reviews: `gdpr-compliance` (a new personal-data-adjacent field on `AccessRight`, registered for export), `audit-trail-completeness`, `documentation-style`.

## The edu teaching dataset under the guidelines

The edu project's [dataset note](../../projects/edu/docs/engineering-notes/anonymised-teaching-dataset.md), in the project's own repository, designs a controlled-access pool of expert-annotated EEG excerpts prepared in the browser at each centre: fixed channel template, fixed sampling rate and ranges, fixed excerpt lengths, no date, no free text, coded events in a sidecar, an age band and a recording class as the only quasi-identifiers, pooled and delayed ingest under a system account, a k-and-m release gate, an embargo and a curator veto. It already reasons from Recital 26, *Breyer* and *EDPS v SRB*, and it already refuses to describe the dataset as anonymised in user-facing text. Read against the guidelines it is the contextual approach (¶ 45–49) applied to a research-style recipient population, which is the case Example 22 (¶ 94) says can succeed. What follows is where it stands under each criterion and what has to change for its position to hold.

**Relevant entities** (¶ 12, 30): learners, contributors, the operator and its staff, the contributing centres, and anyone who obtains the data by breach or by a learner forwarding it. The centre is the entity that holds the manifest, so for the centre the data is personal by inspection and stays so (¶ 37); the finding the design is after is anonymity *for learners and for the operator*.

**No Record Isolation** fails, as it does for every recording: each excerpt is unique in its samples. The follow-up question (¶ 98) is whether a learner can single a record out by matching it to something they hold, which is the linkage question.

**No Linkage** is where the design does its work, and where the platform currently undermines it. The profile removes every header-level and metadata-level quasi-identifier the platform's own ingest leaves behind (length, sampling rate, ranges, channel set), and pooling hides the centre. What remains are the two platform-created keys the assessment found: `file_hash`, which today makes the API a membership oracle for anyone holding an export, and `created_at` with creation-ordered listings, which the note itself names as a leak to close. Phase 1 closes both platform-wide; the dataset additionally serves the release month in place of any upload date and releases on a monthly cadence that bunches a month's submissions from every centre (Phase 7). With those closed, a learner's linkage means reduce to obtaining the centre's manifest or a reference recording of a known subject, which are the two residual attacks the note names, bounded by hidden provenance, embargo and the access controls.

**No Inference.** The clinical inferences a learner draws are the dataset's purpose. They violate the criterion only if they attach to an identifiable person (¶ 67, 87; Example 20): with linkage closed for the learner population they do not, and the k-gate on the quasi-identifier tuple is what keeps the metadata from re-opening it. Membership inference against an enrolment recording stays as the residual the note already carries.

**What in the design is not yet up to the guideline:**

1. **The position wording.** The project settings and README state that "no patient personal data reaches the platform" and that recordings are "anonymised in the submitting clinician's browser". Under ¶ 40 and ¶ 37 the defensible statement is narrower: the platform receives de-identified excerpts; whether they are anonymous for learners and the operator is a finding of the release gate plus the documented assessment, made per release, and the excerpts remain personal data for the centre that holds the manifest. The browser tool de-identifies; nothing in a browser can anonymise on its own, because anonymity is a property of the recipient population, not of the bytes (¶ 7, 22).
2. **Learners must be authenticated individuals, never join-link holders.** Today's teaching sessions reach students through a shared student user and a `public_share_token` join link, both with `apply_middleware=False`. A forwardable, unauthenticated link fails the onward-transfer test (¶ 21) outright and gives no per-recipient accountability, which is what the access-control argument at ¶ 89 rests on. Dataset members must resolve for individual accounts only; the visibility gate in Phase 7 refuses share-token callers for them. The existing session mechanics can stay for material that is not part of the dataset.
3. **Contract is a complement, not the control** (¶ 34, 90). The learner terms (no re-identification attempts, no redistribution) belong in the assessment as a factor reducing likelihood, with the technical limits carrying the weight: authenticated access, the per-request `Activity` row the middleware already writes, the API throttle, and no bulk endpoint. The note's phrase "viewer-only with no bulk export" is right; "no download" would not be, because the viewer fetches the bytes and a learner can keep them. Say what is enforced.
4. **Mixed data must stay separated** (¶ 36). Unreleased members are hidden by the visibility gate; a member the curator vetoes must be purged through the withdrawal path, not left hidden, or the pool holds a record the assessment does not cover.
5. **No motivation arguments** (¶ 31). The note's insider adversary model is the right frame; a future revision must not add "students have no reason to" reasoning.
6. **Documentation and reassessment** (¶ 41, 35, 42). The anonymity report the note plans per release is the ¶ 41 artefact; add to it the per-recording record from Phase 3 and the profile version, keep it with each release, and re-run it on every release run and every profile change. A manifest compromise at a centre is a ¶ 42 incident for that centre's contributions, so the participation agreement needs a notification duty and the operator runbook a reassessment step.
7. **Withdrawal survives Phase 1, and the purge report is the only confirmation.** The note keys withdrawal on `file_hash`; Phase 1 removes the field from every response, not from the row, and the purge command reads the row. The command's per-hash report, purged or not found, handed to the requesting centre by the operator, is the only answer the platform gives to whether a hash exists. No lookup is built for any role, contributors included: an endpoint that answers for a hash the caller holds is the membership oracle the note forbids, whoever the caller is.
8. **The acquisition embargo has no fixed length.** The note's profile carries a minimum recording age and named five years as the starting point. The right length depends on the pool: a Europe-wide project with dozens of contributing centres dilutes provenance far more than a national cohort with half a dozen, so the value is the deployment's, set in the profile and justified in the per-release assessment, and the platform fixes no number. What is fixed is that the field exists and the gate checks the attestation.
9. **One residual is narrower than written.** The note lists annotation rows carrying creation timestamps and an annotator string as a leak. The annotation serialisers do not emit `created_at` or `modified_at`, and the `annotator` value accepted from share-token writers is not stored, so nothing on that surface reaches a learner today; only the recording-side `created_at` and ordering remain, and Phase 1 and Phase 7 close them.

The prerequisites for the dataset's first release are Phases 1, 2 and 3 and the dataset-specific work in Phase 7; Phases 4 to 6 reduce the surface the assessment has to defend and are worth having before the pool is opened to a second institution.

## Phase 7 — Dataset support

The platform-side items from the edu note's "work required", restated as platform features so a second project could use them:

- **A validating ingest path.** An upload variant that checks a submission against a registered profile (channel set and order, sampling rate and ranges, fixed length, blanked identification fields, no annotation TALs, sidecar schema with forbidden keys refused, declared hash equal to the received bytes) and rejects before any row or file is written. Today a rejected file lands as a FAILED recording until purge, which is the wrong outcome for bytes that might carry identifying content. The profile registry is the project's; the gate hook is the platform's, beside the existing conversion hooks in [recordings/README.md](../../recordings/README.md#conversion-hooks).
- **Batch target and pooled ingest.** The upload's `Activity` row targets a batch object; a delayed task ingests across batches in randomised order under `with_system_activity` with a system author and writes no batch identifier onto the row.
- **A read-visibility gate for unreleased members**, registered with `register_read_visibility_gate` in [epicurrents/permissions.py](../../epicurrents/permissions.py), which also refuses share-token callers for any dataset member. Endpoint-side 404 shaping follows the FAILED-hidden pattern.
- **Monthly release runs, and a release month in place of any upload date.** A recording uploaded in month M becomes eligible at the run at the start of M+2, so every member waits between one and two months and a month's submissions from every centre surface together, with the k, m and curator conditions applied to the eligible set. One regime for the whole pool, historical backlog and new submissions alike: two regimes would make the release month a quasi-identifier for which kind a recording is. Listing and detail surfaces serve the release month and a stable random order; Phase 1's month truncation is the platform-wide floor, and the dataset replaces the value entirely. The acquisition embargo attested in the sidecar is a separate control whose length the deployment sets in the profile.
- **The assessment record on the release run.** Each run stores the profile version, the `deidentification_version` range of what it released, the k and m values in force, the curator sign-offs and a reference to the written assessment, so the ¶ 41 record for the dataset lives with the release. Phase 6's per-grant fields are the general-sharing counterpart and are not used for dataset members.
- **`purge_dataset_recordings`** keyed on `file_hash`, running under the audited system scope, unlinking the file, deleting the row, recording the deletion, and printing per submitted hash whether it was purged or not found. That report is the withdrawal receipt the operator returns to the centre and the platform's only answer to whether a hash exists; no endpoint answers it. This is the dataset's Art. 17 path, since its recordings belong to no platform user.
- **A project check** refusing to boot with `RECORDINGS_ORIGINALS_PATH` set on a dataset deployment.
- **Content pinning on the serving endpoints.** An optional `expect_stored_hash` query parameter on the byte-serving and slice endpoints, answering 412 when the recording's `stored_hash` differs. `stored_hash` moves whenever the platform rewrites the file and stays put across metadata edits, which `content_hash` does not, so a release manifest, a dataset snapshot or a cached analysis can carry it and fail loudly on a reprocessed recording instead of reading different bytes under the same URL. Not dataset-specific, and small enough to ship with the first slice.
- **An access report** for the assessment: per dataset member, the count of distinct readers and requests over a window, derived from `Activity` rows. This is the evidence for the ¶ 89 access-control argument and for the six-monthly sweep.
- Reviews: `phi-exposure`, `audit-trail-completeness`, `csrf-coverage` (new upload endpoint), `gdpr-compliance` (new batch model, new purge path), `load-bearing-diff-reviewer` (the visibility-gate registration touches [epicurrents/permissions.py](../../epicurrents/permissions.py)), `documentation-style`.

## Sequencing and size

| Phase | Depends on | Size | Ships alone? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Vocabulary | — | S | Shipped |
| 1 Linkage keys + shape test | — | M | Shipped 2026-09-22 |
| 2 Model default | — | S | Yes |
| 3 Per-recording record | 1 (shape test) | M | Yes |
| 4 Free-text hygiene | 1 (shape test, for `warnings`) | M | Yes, or split: tag scope and push bodies first, the heuristic second |
| 5 Export labelling | 3 (version range in the envelope) | S | Yes |

| 7 Dataset support | 1, 2 (default), 3 (record) | L | In slices: gate, purge and release cadence first, validating ingest and pooling second, the reports last |

Phases 1 and 2 are the minimum before any deployment states its position under the guidelines to a recipient; phases 3 and 6 are what let it document that position; phases 4 and 5 reduce the surface the position has to defend. Phase 7 is what the edu dataset needs on top, and its first slice (the visibility gate, the share-token refusal, the purge command and the monthly release cadence) is the release blocker. None of them changes the classification of the output, which stays pseudonymised personal data, and the assessment's given-data tables are updated in the commit that ships each phase.

## Versioning

None of the phases touches the compatibility surface in [epicurrents/README.md → Versioning and the platform pin](../../epicurrents/README.md#versioning-and-the-platform-pin) in a breaking way: the middleware ABCs are unchanged, the old class name stays importable, model names and identity fields are unchanged. Phase 2 changes behaviour for project code that creates grants without the flag, which is a CHANGELOG entry under the current patch line rather than a minor bump. If a later decision removes the `AnonymizeEDFHeader` alias, that is the breaking change and takes the minor bump on `0.x`.
