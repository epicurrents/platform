# Embedded radiology view — plan

Decided 2026-11-21. The dicom plugin opens a study in a new tab running standalone OHIF, and the viewer edition's radiology view loads OHIF from a hard-coded bundle name under `/ohif/` that no deployment serves. This plan makes a DICOM study a resource of the signed-in viewer like a recording or a media file, opened in the radiology view, so a recording and the ultrasound study attached to it (the combined NCS + US case) sit in one dataset sidebar. The ROADMAP entry it replaces is the DICOM plugin's "Embedded radiology view"; the plugin's reference is [plugins/dicom/README.md](../../plugins/dicom/README.md).

## Status

Built on 2026-11-21, phases 1 to 6, across the interface (the `rad` module, the framing `RadiologyInterface`, the footer trail, `switchView` with its crossfade and its restore), the builder (the `rad` registrar, the `full` profile, the OHIF clone, copy and convert steps removed) and the platform. Phase 6 drove every route in Chrome against the dev stack under the enforced production policy, with cross-origin isolation on in the viewer and in the frame: a study opened from the study list, an attached ultrasound study listed after its recording and back again through OHIF's Back button, a CT study added to a dataset from the study list and opened from it, and the theme crossfade measured on screenshots; no policy violation was reported by either document. Where the build departed from the design below: the attachments listing became a core registry (decision of the same day, recorded under [Attachments](#attachments)) rather than a study-list filter; building the dataset route found a live gap, a study stored without the pass served raw through a de-identifying dataset grant, closed by a resolver step (`register_terms_refusal`) instead of per-surface checks; the dataset author is not exempt from the listing's de-identifying filter, since their own dataset row carries `apply_middleware` at its default. The plugin README is the reference now: [plugins/dicom/README.md → Embedded view](../../plugins/dicom/README.md#embedded-view).

## Decisions

| # | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How the edition embeds OHIF | **A same-origin iframe** on the plugin's OHIF mount, not OHIF's scripts injected into the SPA document. OHIF's main bundle compiles code with `new Function`, which the SPA's `script-src` refuses; it also brings a global stylesheet, a router reading the page URL, document-wide hotkeys and no teardown API. In an iframe all of that stays in OHIF's own document under the mount's own policy |
| 2 | How a study reaches the view | All three: attached to a recording, as a dataset member, and from the study list (`?dicom=<hash>` on the SPA viewer). The standalone mount stays |
| 3 | Later, not now | A side-by-side view in the NCS module, signals on one side and the ultrasound study on the other. An earlier attempt with an older Cornerstone in the viewer document failed on Cornerstone binding its configuration to globals and allowing no second instance; the likely route is the same iframe isolation, a small Cornerstone page on the plugin mount framed inside the NCS view, which gives each instance a document of its own. It goes in the ROADMAP |

## Design

### The edition

The interface gains a `rad` module on the native media module's pattern ([interface/src/app/modules/native/](../../frontend/viewer/interface/src/app/modules/native/)): a `RadiologyResource` holding the address of a viewer page, a loader that makes one from a URL without fetching it, and `registerRadiology`, which registers the module and the importer `rad/viewer-url`. The edition knows nothing about DICOM or OHIF: the host hands it a URL that renders a study, and the platform's is the OHIF mount's `viewer/dicomjson?url=<ohif-json>`.

[RadiologyInterface.vue](../../frontend/viewer/interface/src/app/views/radiology/RadiologyInterface.vue) becomes an iframe pointed at the active radiology resource's URL. The view is already mounted once and hidden while inactive ([App.vue](../../frontend/viewer/interface/src/app/App.vue)), so the iframe is created when a radiology resource is first activated and survives a switch to the biosignal view and back with OHIF's state intact. Opening another study replaces the iframe's address, so one OHIF instance exists at a time. The script tags, the `PUBLIC_URL` global, the height override and the builder's `convert.mjs` and copy steps for OHIF go.

The builder gains [setup/modules/rad.ts](../../frontend/viewer/setup/modules/rad.ts), a registry entry, and `rad` in the full profile's `activeModules`. `activeViews` gates nothing in the interface (it is declared and never read), so no setup change turns the view on: activating a `rad` resource selects the view through the module's `compatibleView`.

### The platform

- **Framing.** Every OHIF mount response carries `frame-ancestors 'self'` in place of the platform's `'none'`, and `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`, beside the `'unsafe-eval'` the mount already adds. No other response changes. The iframe is same-origin, so it inherits the session cookie and the cross-origin isolation headers the platform sets everywhere when `ENABLE_CROSS_ORIGIN_ISOLATION` is on.
- **Viewer item sources.** [ViewerView.vue](../../frontend/src/views/ViewerView.vue) knows recordings and media files and must not learn DICOM. A `ViewerPlugin` gains `viewerItems`, the plugin's answer to three questions: the items a query parameter names (`?dicom=`), the items attached to a recording, and how to describe a dataset member of a type it owns. Each answer is a `StudyDescription` (loader, name, source, properties), which is what the viewer lists. The merged plugin chains the sources of every plugin.
- **Attached studies.** `GET /dicom/studies/?attached_to_type=recording&attached_to_id=<hash>` lists the studies attached to a recording the caller can read, through the attachment rule in [plugins/dicom/permissions.py](../../plugins/dicom/permissions.py), so a study stored as uploaded stays withheld from a de-identifying grant. The viewer lists them after their recording, in the same dataset.
- **Dataset members.** The dataset item listing names a study today by its sequential primary key with no type and no hash. The library gains an item-type registry: a plugin registers its model with the `object_type` it is listed as, how its hash is read and resolved, and which rows are live, and the listing, the hash resolution of an item write and `_listable_items` consult it. A study is refused in a release-gated dataset, and gating a dataset holding one is refused, because the release gate covers recordings and media only. The study list gains "Add to dataset".
- **De-identifying grants beyond attachment.** A dataset grant passes its `apply_middleware` to its members, so a study stored as uploaded (`deidentification_version` null) read under such a grant is withheld on every study surface, as the attachment rule already does for attachments.
- **Share tokens.** The study endpoints authenticate by session only, so a dataset opened with a share token lists no study.

## Attachments

Decided 2026-11-21, replacing a study-list filter and a plugin-only source.

**The boundary.** A biosignal recording is de-identified by the platform, at ingest and again on the wire, and a de-identifying grant changes what its reader receives: that is a promise the platform makes and the assessment rests on. An attachment (video, audio, image, document, DICOM study) is content its submitter answers for in full, metadata included, and is expected to arrive de-identified: for a DICOM study, preferably by the clinical software that produces the export, which knows the source system's fields and can apply a profile before anything leaves the site. Where the platform cleans an attachment's container, as the DICOM pass cleans the header, that is defence in depth and not a promise of de-identification: it is recorded as what was done, never presented as what the reader can rely on. A de-identifying grant changes nothing in an attachment's bytes; the one thing the platform does under such a grant is withhold what it knows to be raw, as it withholds a DICOM study stored without the pass. No surface may describe an attachment as de-identified by the platform. The DICOM docs and notices are re-read against this in phase 6.

**The registry.** [epicurrents/attachments.py](../../epicurrents/attachments.py) holds `register_attachment_type(key, ...)`, called from each owning app's `ready()`: `media` registers `mediafile`, the dicom plugin `dicomstudy`. A registration names the model, the live rows attached to a parent, how an item is described, the pass the platform applied to it, as a record rather than an assurance (`platform_pass`: `none` or `dicom-header`) and whether its serving endpoints accept a share token. Core applies `can_read_object` to every row, so each type's own attachment extension decides (the DICOM withholding rule included), and a type whose endpoints are session-only is left out for a share-token caller rather than listed unopenable.

**The listing.** `GET /recordings/api/v1/attachments/?recordings=<hash>,…` (up to 200, one dataset browser page) answers every attachment the caller may read on each named recording the caller may read, each item carrying its `parent` hash, `type`, `hash`, display name, the fields its viewer importer needs and `platform_pass`. One request per page, rather than one per recording, is what keeps a large dataset cheap. Hidden recordings (`_hidden_for_caller`) list nothing. The audit row carries counts.

**The viewer.** Attached videos keep hanging on their recording for synchronised playback. Every other attachment is listed after its recording, through the media importers for media and through a `ViewerItemSource.describeAttachment` for a plugin's type, so the nested navigator can later list it under the parent the item names.

## Phases

1. **The view.** The interface module and the iframe, the builder registrar and profile, the framing headers. Verified by opening a study's viewer URL through `rad/viewer-url` in the dev-built edition.
2. **The way back, and the theme.** The radiology view is dark and every other view light; a view switch that changes the theme crossfades the page through the View Transitions API, one layer for every component (an instant switch is harsh on the eyes, and transitioning each element's colours, as the Nextcloud app did, runs at a different pace per component and per Web Awesome part). A theme chosen per resource, which the side-by-side view will need, is an interface ROADMAP item. The radiology view hides the dataset navigator, since OHIF has a study panel of its own and needs the width; opening a resource now applies a view's component settings, which it skipped before, and leaving the view restores what the user had. Opened from a dataset, a study therefore needs a route back to it. The radiology view shows the bottom bar, where the `rad` module's footer names the dataset and the study as a trail, as the tabular resource's breadcrumb did, and a step of the trail reactivates the resource the user came from. Inside OHIF, the app-config's `whiteLabeling.createLogoComponentFn` replaces the logo with a back button when the page is framed (`window.parent !== window`), which posts a same-origin message the radiology view answers the same way; the standalone mount keeps the logo. Built after phase 1 has been seen in the browser, since the menubar and footer decide the room OHIF has.
3. **From the study list.** The `viewerItems` source on `ViewerPlugin` and its merge, `?dicom=` in [ViewerView.vue](../../frontend/src/views/ViewerView.vue), and "Open" in the study list opening the SPA viewer.
4. **Attachments.** Built in core rather than in the plugin, so the viewer lists everything attached to a recording the same way; see [Attachments](#attachments).
5. **Dataset members.** The item-type registry, the gated-dataset refusal, the de-identifying-grant rule, "Add to dataset" and the dataset source.
6. **End to end.** A recording with an attached ultrasound study and a dataset holding a CT study, driven in Chrome against the dev stack under the production CSP; then docs, ROADMAP and the handover.

## Open points

- OHIF's own header links to its study list, which inside the iframe replaces the study. The phase 2 logo replacement removes the link when framed.
- The viewer's keyboard shortcuts do not reach a focused iframe. That is the intended isolation, but the shortcut that switches datasets will not work until focus leaves OHIF.
